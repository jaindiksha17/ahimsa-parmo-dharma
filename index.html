PRAYER & REFLECTION 4 MIN READ

The Navkar Mantra: a prayer of reverence

A beloved Jain prayer honours awakened souls and the qualities they embody.

Read the article ↗

The Navkar, or Namokar, Mantra is among the most widely recited prayers in Jainism. Rather than asking for a favour, it offers respectful salutations to five spiritual ideals: the arihants, siddhas, acharyas, upadhyayas, and all sadhus and sadhvis.

णमो अरिहंताणं
णमो सिद्धाणं
णमो आयरियाणं
णमो उवज्झायाणं
णमो लोए सव्वसाहूणं

एसो पंच णमोक्कारो, सव्वपावप्पणासणो।
मंगलाणं च सव्वेसिं, पढमं हवइ मंगलं॥

The mantra points attention toward qualities such as wisdom, discipline, and liberation. Its words are traditionally understood across Jain communities, though pronunciation, transliteration, and personal practice may vary. Many people recite it as part of prayer, meditation, or a quiet moment of reflection.

PLACES & PILGRIMAGE 4 MIN READ

Palitana: a pilgrimage taken step by step

On Shatrunjaya Hill, a long climb leads pilgrims through a remarkable temple landscape.

Read the article ↗

Rising above the city of Palitana in Gujarat, Shatrunjaya Hill is one of Jainism’s most revered pilgrimage places. Its slopes hold hundreds of temples, shaped over centuries by the devotion of Jain communities.

For many pilgrims, the climb itself is part of the experience: a chance to move with intention, visit sacred spaces, and share the journey with others. People make the ascent in different ways and for different reasons. The path is not a single prescribed experience, and practices may differ across communities and individuals.

Palitana offers a glimpse of how a sacred landscape can hold history, architecture, and living devotion together. If you visit, learn about local customs ahead of time, dress respectfully, and follow guidance from the community and temple caretakers.

ART & ARCHITECTURE 3 MIN READ

Reading the details of a Jain temple

Carved pillars and gateways tell stories of devotion, craftsmanship, and care.

Read the article ↗

Jain temples across India reflect the communities, materials, and artistic traditions that shaped them. Some are known for intricately carved marble; others for stone gateways, quiet courtyards, or the artistry of their shrines.

Look closely and architecture becomes an invitation to pay attention: to repeating patterns, the patience behind the craft, and the way light moves through a space. The temples are not just historic monuments. They are places of worship and community life today.

There is no single style that represents all Jain traditions. Each temple has its own history and customs; visitors can honour that by following posted guidance and asking before photographing worshippers or sacred spaces.

THE THREE JEWELS · RATNATRAYA

A way of becoming

A path shaped
by intention.

In Jainism, the Three Jewels — right faith, right knowledge, and right conduct — are a guide toward liberation. They are not separate steps, but qualities that grow together through reflection and practice.

01

Right faith
An open, discerning trust in the possibility of awakening.

02

Right knowledge
Learning to see things as they are, beyond assumption.

03

Right conduct
Letting understanding shape a more compassionate life.
